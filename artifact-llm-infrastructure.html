Generative AI LLM Infrastructure: Training Process & Costs

A visual guide to the journey from data collection to model deployment—and the resources that make large language models possible.

Important: Frontier-model training costs are often confidential. This artifact distinguishes public disclosures from information not confirmed by the cited sources; outside estimates should not be mistaken for official costs.

The training pipeline

A simplified lifecycle. Teams iterate between training, evaluation, and improvement; deployment also requires separate ongoing infrastructure.

01 · Source

Collect & license data

Gather text and, where relevant, image, audio, or video data. Consider quality, permissions, privacy, provenance, and representation.

Data accessRights review

Clean & tokenize

Filter and deduplicate content, organize datasets, and convert material into tokens the model can process.

StorageData pipelines
03 · Pretrain

Learn language patterns

Train a neural network to predict the next token across repeated batches, while tracking model quality, hardware use, and failures.

Fine-tune & evaluate

Package & deploy

Prepare the model for an application or API, optimize serving, add safeguards, and monitor reliability and latency.

Four resource budgets

The bars are illustrative emphasis only—not measured cost shares for a particular model.

01 · Data

Quality, rights & coverage

Acquisition · preparation · governance

Licensing, cleaning, labeling, deduplication, and storage influence both cost and model quality. Data diversity and provenance matter.

Costs vary with data source, licensing terms, modality, and preparation effort.

02 · Compute

Accelerators × time × utilization

Hardware · network · engineering

Large clusters require accelerators, memory, interconnects, storage, and software. Longer runs and additional experiments need more capacity.

GPU-hours alone omit cluster rental or ownership, networking, retries, and staff.

03 · Energy & facilities

Power for computing and cooling

Measured power × run duration

Energy depends on hardware, utilization, run length, and data-center efficiency. Location and electricity mix affect price and environmental impact.

Credible estimates need measured power and facility overhead—not parameter count alone.

04 · Time & people

Calendar time plus expert work

Research · iteration · operations

The end-to-end schedule includes data work, training runs, evaluation, failed experiments, and deployment—not only the final successful run.

Team size, iteration, hardware availability, and reliability all affect timelines.

What public examples reveal

Published sources vary in detail. Reporting what is known—and what is not—is part of responsible technical communication.

Example · Meta

Llama 3

Meta’s announcement and technical report describe the Llama 3 models and report pretraining on over 15 trillion tokens. They do not provide a complete financial training bill.

Do not infer an official dollar cost from model size or token count.

Example · OpenAI

GPT‑4

The GPT‑4 Technical Report states that it does not provide details such as model size, hardware, training compute, or dataset construction.

Specific public cost figures should not be presented as company-confirmed without a primary source.

Explanatory document & design rationale

This visual explains the LLM training lifecycle from collecting and preparing data through pretraining, alignment, evaluation, deployment, and ongoing operation. It highlights data, compute, energy, time, and expert labor as connected resource categories that influence project feasibility and model quality.

The key consideration is transparency: organizations do not disclose the same level of infrastructure or cost information. For that reason, the model examples separate reported facts from unavailable information and avoid presenting speculative estimates as official figures.

The numbered pipeline communicates sequence, while four resource cards make cost drivers easy to scan. A comparison table supports direct reading of public disclosures. Responsive layouts, clear headings, accessible color contrast, keyboard focus, reduced-motion support, and print styling are used to make the artifact easier to access and share.

Skills demonstrated: AI infrastructure literacy, research synthesis, responsible communication, systems thinking, and accessible visual presentation.
